Executive SummaryThe Department of Social Justice & Empowerment (DoSJE), under the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment (MoSJE), Government of India, serves millions of citizens across diverse beneficiary groups — Scheduled Castes, Other Backward Classes, senior citizens, persons with disabilities, transgender individuals, drug de-addiction beneficiaries, and sanitation workers. To improve citizen experience, reduce call centre load, and ensure 24×7 availability of scheme information, the Department proposes to deploy a state-of-the-art AI-powered chatbot on the official portal.This Business Requirement Document (BRD) defines the functional scope, technical specifications, UI/UX guidelines, data governance requirements, integration architecture, and non-functional requirements for the chatbot solution. The chatbot — internally codenamed SAHAY (Smart AI Helper for Assistance & Yojana information) — will serve as an interactive, multilingual, context-aware digital assistant capable of answering citizen queries related to all 17+ flagship schemes of MOSJE.Key Objectives at a GlanceProvide instant, accurate, and verifiable information on all MOSJE schemes.Reduce dependency on manual helplines and in-person enquiries.Offer an accessible, multilingual, mobile-first interface.Maintain full conversation history with export/import capabilities.Deliver source-cited responses for transparency and trust.Enable future scalability for new schemes and integrations.2. Project Background & Motivation2.1 About the DepartmentThe Department of Social Justice & Empowerment (DoSJE) is the nodal department for welfare, empowerment, and social justice for historically disadvantaged communities in India. Its mandate covers policy formulation, scheme implementation, grant disbursement, and oversight across five broad pillars:Welfare of Scheduled Castes (SCs)Welfare of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) and Economically Backward Classes (EBCs)Welfare of Senior CitizensPrevention of Alcoholism and Drug AbuseWelfare of Transgender Persons and Persons Engaged in Begging2.2 Current ChallengesDespite offering a wide array of schemes, DoSJE faces significant challenges in citizen outreach and information dissemination:Information Fragmentation: Scheme information is scattered across 17+ separate portals and PDFs, making navigation difficult for ordinary citizens.Language Barrier: A large portion of beneficiaries communicate in regional languages and face difficulty accessing English-only content.Helpline Overload: National helplines (14446, 14566, 14567) receive thousands of repetitive informational queries that could be automated.Digital Literacy Gap: Many potential beneficiaries are unaware of schemes applicable to them.No Single Window: Citizens must visit multiple websites to understand eligibility, documentation, and application procedures.2.3 Opportunity StatementA next-generation AI chatbot on the MOSJE portal can bridge this gap — acting as a single intelligent interface that guides citizens to the right scheme, explains eligibility and documentation requirements, provides real-time helpline numbers, and refers users to the appropriate application portals. This aligns with the Government of India's broader Digital India initiative and the National e-Governance Plan (NeGP).3. Functional Requirements4.1 Core Conversational EngineFR-CONV: Conversation ManagementFR-CONV-01: The chatbot shall understand and respond to natural language queries in English and Hindi (with auto-detect). The system shall support intent recognition, entity extraction, and contextual disambiguation.FR-CONV-02: The system shall maintain a conversation context window of at least 20 previous exchanges per session. All responses shall consider the full conversation history to avoid repetition and improve relevance.FR-CONV-03: The chatbot shall gracefully handle out-of-scope queries by acknowledging limitations and suggesting related topics or directing the user to the DoSJE contact page.FR-CONV-04: The system shall support multi-turn clarification dialogues — e.g., if a user asks 'Am I eligible?', the chatbot shall ask follow-up questions (age, caste category, income) before answering.FR-CONV-05: Responses shall be structured — using bullet points, headings, numbered lists, tables, and call-to-action buttons where appropriate — to maximise clarity.4.2 Scheme Knowledge & Q&A EngineFR-SCHEME: Scheme-Specific CapabilitiesFR-SCHEME-01: The chatbot shall have a deep knowledge base for each of the 17 listed schemes including: scheme overview, objectives, eligibility criteria, benefits/quantum of assistance, application procedure, required documents, implementing agency, portal links, helpline numbers, FAQs, and recent updates.FR-SCHEME-02: Users shall be able to ask free-form questions such as 'How do I apply for PM-DAKSH?', 'Who is eligible for NOS Scholarship?', 'What documents do I need for Post Matric Scholarship?', or 'What is the PM-AJAY scheme?'.FR-SCHEME-03: The knowledge base shall be updateable by authorized administrators through a CMS-style interface without code changes.FR-SCHEME-04: The chatbot shall support scheme comparison queries — e.g., 'What is the difference between PM-DAKSH and TAPAS?'.FR-SCHEME-05: For schemes with online application portals, the chatbot shall display a direct 'Apply Now' button linking to the appropriate government portal.4.3 Chat History & MemoryFR-HIST: Persistent History ManagementFR-HIST-01: All conversation history within a session shall be stored in the browser's local storage (or server-side for authenticated users) and remain accessible on page reload.FR-HIST-02: Users shall be able to view a chronological list of all past chat sessions, each labelled with date, time, and a system-generated session summary.FR-HIST-03: Users shall be able to continue any previous chat session, with the chatbot restoring full context and treating it as an active conversation.FR-HIST-04: Users shall be able to delete individual sessions or clear all history from the UI.FR-HIST-05: The system shall support session tagging — users can label sessions with custom names (e.g., 'My PM-DAKSH query').4.4 Export & Import FeatureFR-EXPORT: Chat Session PortabilityFR-EXPORT-01: Users shall be able to export any chat session in two formats: (a) JSON file containing the full conversation data structure, and (b) formatted PDF with the MOSJE header, date-time stamps, and source citations clearly laid out.FR-EXPORT-02: The PDF export shall be auto-formatted with: page headers carrying the MOSJE logo and session date, colour-coded message bubbles (blue for user, green for assistant), source citations in footnotes, and a session summary at the top.FR-EXPORT-03: Users shall be able to import a previously exported JSON file to resume a past session. The system shall validate file integrity before importing.FR-EXPORT-04: Bulk export of all sessions shall be available as a ZIP archive of individual JSON files.FR-EXPORT-05: Imported sessions shall be marked with an 'Imported' badge to distinguish them from native sessions.4.5 Onboarding & Suggested QuestionsFR-ONBOARD: New User GuidanceFR-ONBOARD-01: For first-time users and new sessions, the chatbot shall display a welcome screen with: the MOSJE mission statement, a brief chatbot introduction, and a curated set of 8–12 suggested starter questions across scheme categories.FR-ONBOARD-02: Suggested questions shall be categorised by beneficiary type (e.g., 'I am a student', 'I am a senior citizen', 'I run an NGO', 'I am seeking drug de-addiction help').FR-ONBOARD-03: Each category card shall be clickable to auto-populate the relevant question into the input field and trigger an immediate response.FR-ONBOARD-04: Dynamic suggested questions shall also appear mid-conversation based on context — e.g., after answering a query about PM-AJAY, the chatbot shall suggest: 'Would you like to know the application process?' or 'Check your eligibility for PM-AJAY'.FR-ONBOARD-05: The chatbot shall display a 'Not sure? Tell me who you are' button that launches a guided onboarding questionnaire (3–5 questions) to identify the most relevant schemes.4.6 Source Citation SystemFR-CITE: Transparency & ReferenceFR-CITE-01: Every factual response generated by the AI shall include a 'Sources' section at the bottom listing the references used — including: source document name (e.g., 'PM-DAKSH Scheme Guidelines 2023-24'), source URL (official portal or PDF link), last updated date of the source, and source type (Official Gazette / Scheme Guidelines / FAQ / Annual Report).FR-CITE-02: Sources shall be displayed in a collapsible accordion component that is closed by default but easily expandable, maintaining UI cleanliness.FR-CITE-03: Each source shall be a hyperlink to the original government document, opening in a new tab.FR-CITE-04: If the system cannot identify a reliable source, the response shall carry a clear disclaimer: 'This information is based on general knowledge of scheme guidelines and may not reflect the latest updates. Please verify at [portal URL].'FR-CITE-05: Admins shall be able to maintain a curated source library mapping each scheme to its official documents, ensuring citation accuracy.4.7 Multilingual SupportFR-LANG-01: The interface shall support English, Hindi, and 8 additional Indian languages: Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Urdu, Punjabi, Kashmiri and Odia.FR-LANG-02: Language selection shall be available via a prominent toggle at the top of the chat window. The system shall also auto-detect the user's browser language as a default.FR-LANG-03: All UI labels, placeholder text, onboarding content, and scheme FAQs shall be pre-translated and stored. Dynamic AI responses shall be translated in real time using a translation API.4.8 AccessibilityFR-ACC-01: The chatbot shall comply with WCAG 2.1 Level AA and the GoI Web Accessibility Guidelines (GIGW 3.0).FR-ACC-02: Full keyboard navigation, screen reader compatibility (ARIA labels), and high-contrast mode shall be supported.FR-ACC-03: Text font size shall be adjustable (small / medium / large) within the chat interface.5. UI/UX Design Requirements5.1 Design PhilosophyThe MOSJE chatbot interface shall embody a futuristic yet approachable design language — reflecting the trustworthiness and authority of a Government of India platform while being welcoming to diverse citizens. The design system shall draw from:GoI Tricolour identity (deep navy, vibrant saffron, forest green)Glassmorphism and subtle gradient effects for modern depthSmooth micro-animations for interactive feedbackClean typographic hierarchy using accessible, multilingual-friendly fontsFloating, non-intrusive layout that overlays on the main portal5.2 Chat Widget ModesThe chatbot shall operate in two display modes:Floating Bubble Mode: A minimised circular avatar/button (bottom-right corner) with a pulsating animation and an unread badge. Clicking expands the full chat panel.Expanded Panel Mode: A full-height side panel (420px width on desktop, full-screen on mobile) with smooth slide-in animation, containing the header, conversation thread, input area, and action toolbar.5.3 Visual Design SpecificationsComponentSpecificationDetailsChat HeaderHeight: 64pxGradient from navy (#003087) to saffron (#FF6600). Technical Architecture6.1 High-Level ArchitectureThe MOSJE chatbot follows a modern, microservices-inspired architecture consisting of four primary layers:Frontend Layer: React.js / Next.js based progressive web application with Tailwind CSS for styling, deployed on NIC cloud or approved GoI cloud infrastructure.API Gateway Layer: RESTful API gateway (Node.js / FastAPI) managing session tokens, rate limiting, authentication, and routing to downstream services.AI Inference Layer: LLM integration (GPT-4 / Claude / Gemini via API, or NeSi-approved open-source model) with a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline for scheme-specific accuracy.Data Layer: PostgreSQL for session/history storage, Redis for session caching, Elasticsearch / vector database (Pinecone / Weaviate) for scheme knowledge base retrieval.6.2 RAG Architecture for Scheme KnowledgeGiven the critical need for accuracy and traceability in government information, the chatbot shall use Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):All scheme guidelines, FAQs, and official documents shall be ingested, chunked, and embedded into a vector database.On each user query, semantically relevant chunks are retrieved and injected into the LLM prompt as context.The LLM generates a response grounded in the retrieved documents, enabling source citation.A feedback loop allows administrators to flag incorrect responses and refine embeddings.6.3 Technology StackLayerTechnologyRationaleFrontendReact.js 18 / Next.js 14SSR for SEO, excellent performance, Non-Functional Requirements7.1 PerformanceAverage response time for a chatbot reply: ≤ 3 seconds under normal loadPage load time for chat widget: ≤ 1.5 seconds (LCP)Concurrent users supported: ≥ 10,000 simultaneous sessionsSystem availability: 99.9% uptime SLA (excluding GoI scheduled maintenance windows)7.2 SecurityAll API communication over HTTPS/TLS 1.3Input sanitization to prevent prompt injection attacksPII masking: No personally identifiable information stored in logsOWASP Top-10 compliance mandatory for all endpointsData retention policy: Chat history auto-purged after 90 days (configurable by user)CERT-In guidelines compliance for government-facing applications7.3 Scalability & ReliabilityHorizontal auto-scaling on API and AI inference layersCircuit breaker patterns for LLM API failures with graceful fallback to static FAQ modeDaily automated backups of the knowledge base and session databaseCDN-cached static assets with versioned deployments for zero-downtime updates7.4 CompliancePersonal Data Protection compliance aligned with India's DPDP Act 2023GIGW 3.0 — Guidelines for Indian Government WebsitesWCAG 2.1 Level AA — Web Content Accessibility GuidelinesNIC STQC audit requirement for GoI web applications8. Acceptance Criteria & Testing12.1 Functional Acceptance TestsChatbot correctly answers ≥ 95% of 200 pre-defined test questions across all 17 schemesChat history persists across browser sessions and page refreshesExport generates a valid, correctly formatted PDF and JSON fileImported JSON successfully restores conversation context with full message fidelitySource citations are present in ≥ 90% of factual responsesAll 8 regional languages render correctly without character corruptionSuggested questions appear on new session load and dynamically post-response12.2 Performance Acceptance TestsResponse time ≤ 3s for 95th percentile under 5,000 concurrent usersChat widget load time ≤ 1.5s on 4G mobile connectionZero critical or high OWASP vulnerabilities in penetration test99.9% uptime over 30-day stabilisation period post-launch12.3 User Acceptance Testing (UAT)UAT shall involve three user groups: (a) DoSJE officials testing for factual accuracy, (b) NIC technical team testing for security and performance, and (c) citizen volunteers across 5 states testing for usability and language accuracy. UAT sign-off is required from the DoSJE Secretary's office before go-live.13.
